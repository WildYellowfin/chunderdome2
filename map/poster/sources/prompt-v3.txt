Vintage 1930s WPA / National Park screen-print travel poster: a flat, top-down plan-view illustrated map of the Northern California coast from San Francisco north to Dillon Beach at dusk, north up. Only the far north tilts away into three clearly separated receding ridgelines with strong atmospheric value steps: the nearest ridge dark olive, the middle ridge muted teal-grey, the farthest ridge pale hazy peach close to the sky. Above them one coherent dusk sky: a smooth screen-print gradient in three or four soft bands from warm ochre at the horizon to vermilion-orange higher up, with a large low vermilion sun half sunk into the Pacific horizon at upper left and a few short shimmering horizontal reflection strokes on the sea beneath it. Flat layered inks with subtle overprint misregistration and a fine even grain, limited palette: ink #14161b, cream #efe6d4, vermilion #dd4326, deep teal #1d4b57, ochre #d6a03a, olive #6f7440. Deep teal Pacific; lighter teal San Francisco Bay and San Pablo Bay. Land in cream and pale ochre with olive hills shaded in a few large flat stepped bands, not mottled; cream beaches; a few dark cypress silhouettes on headlands. Focal point at the cream half-dome mark: a geodesic dome tent seen from above at three-quarter angle, sitting into the sand dunes of Dillon Beach, triangular facets glowing warm ochre and cream from inside, a small campfire glow beside it, a soft warm halo on the sand around it; same scale, perspective and print style as the rest of the map. No roads, no route lines, no text, letters, numbers, labels, compass rose, cartouche, frame, border, bridge, animals or clip-art.
